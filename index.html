Background

I am a Computer Science graduate from BRAC University, Dhaka, Bangladesh. My research investigates reliability and failure modes in high-stakes clinical machine learning, focusing on patient heterogeneity, early sepsis alerting, and distributed learning.

My core manuscript investigates directional constraints on personalized alert thresholds in early sepsis monitoring using Empirical Bayes methods across 40,336 ICU patients. In my undergraduate thesis, I designed JASAL, an edge-deployable federated learning architecture for physiological anomaly detection with differential privacy. I am also investigating calibration breakdown and prompt sensitivity in medical vision-language models.

My long-term goal is to pursue doctoral research in machine learning, developing robust and verifiable decision systems for healthcare and high-consequence domains.

Lead Research Manuscript

Directional Constraints for Patient-Specific Alert Thresholds in Early Sepsis Monitoring: An Empirical Bayes Analysis

Juboraj Ahmed · Completed Manuscript; preparing for journal submission 2026

This study investigates whether adapting alert thresholds to individual ICU patients improves sepsis detection or inadvertently delays intervention by treating early physiological deterioration as benign baseline heterogeneity.

  • Investigated a failure mode of personalized clinical alerting in which early disease-related risk is misinterpreted as stable patient heterogeneity, potentially elevating alert thresholds and suppressing early warnings.
  • Developed and evaluated Directionally Constrained Empirical Bayes (DC-EB) threshold adaptation, restricting upward personalization while retaining patient-specific downward adjustment.
  • Evaluated thresholding strategies on the PhysioNet/CinC 2019 sepsis dataset comprising 40,336 ICU patients, using 8-, 12-, and 24-hour adaptation histories, patient-level data partitions, and 2,000 paired bootstrap replicates.
  • Found that directional constraints mitigated sensitivity and normalized clinical-utility losses associated with unrestricted personalization, although superiority over the optimized Global threshold was not conclusively established.

Undergraduate Thesis

2025 — 2026

JASAL: A Remote Patient Health Monitoring System Driven by Federated Learning

BRAC University · Dhaka, Bangladesh

Developed and evaluated a federated learning framework for physiological anomaly detection using 32,796 patient records distributed across 10 non-IID client partitions. Investigated differential privacy, secure aggregation, adaptive patient profiling, interpretable anomaly detection, model quantization, and communication-efficient deployment.

  • Evaluated a deep autoencoder-based anomaly detection framework across non-IID federated clients.
  • Investigated differential privacy and secure multiparty computation against privacy reconstruction attacks.
  • Optimised the model for edge deployment using INT8 quantisation and communication-efficient training.
